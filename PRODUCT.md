## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: Guan Xiaoyu (Zhang Damao), a product designer and programmer based in Beijing. Uses this site to publish technical tutorials, tool recommendations, project showcases, and occasional cultural essays. Readers are secondary; the site exists first as a personal publishing space.

Readers are fellow developers, designers, and curious internet dwellers. They arrive via search, social links, or direct navigation. They read individual posts or browse recent content. Their context: scanning during a coffee break, debugging a problem, or exploring a linked reference.

## Product Purpose

A personal publishing platform for long-form writing about programming, tools, digital life, and cultural observations. Serves as both a public notebook and a portfolio of thinking. Success means the content is readable, navigable, and the site feels like a natural extension of the author's voice. Not a growth engine, not a content farm.

## Brand Personality

**务实** — Pragmatic. No decoration for decoration's sake. Content and clarity come first.

**新锐** — Sharp. Editorial confidence in typography and layout. Not blog-default, not template-generic.

**温柔** — Warm. The site should feel human and approachable. Not cold minimalism, not harsh contrast. A space that invites reading.

The brand name "Ignore the Blueprint" signals irreverence toward playbooks and templates. WTFPL license reinforces this: the site doesn't take itself too seriously.

## Anti-references

- **Generic tech blog templates**: Medium clones, default Jekyll/Pelican themes, cookie-cutter developer blogs with identical card grids
- **AI-generated pages**: Soulless, formulaic layouts with placeholder aesthetics
- **Pixel art / retro web**: Distracting, cutesy, wrong tone for this content
- **CSDN**: Cluttered, ad-heavy, visually aggressive Chinese tech platforms
- **Reddit**: Information density at the expense of readability; no hierarchy, no calm

## Design Principles

1. **Content commands, chrome disappears** — The reading experience IS the product. Navigation and structural framing recede; the words dominate. Every design choice must improve readability or navigation. If it doesn't serve the content, it doesn't exist.

2. **Editorial confidence** — Typesetting, spacing, and visual hierarchy worthy of a publication (ft.com as the benchmark). Headlines that command attention. Body text that disappears into reading. No blog-default casualness — deliberate typographic choices at every level.

3. **Warm precision** — Sharp, intentional typography rendered in warm tones. Not cold-minimal (no sterile grays, no harsh #000-on-#fff). Not soft-pastel (no rounded corners, no fuzzy spacing). Precision that feels human, not mechanical.

4. **Earn every element** — Each visual element must justify its existence. No decorative flourishes, no gratuitous icons, no filler. If removing it doesn't hurt the reading experience, remove it.

5. **Practitioner's voice** — The design should feel made by someone who builds things: a product designer who codes. Confident but unpretentious. Competent but not corporate. The site is the author's tool, not a marketing department's output.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific WCAG conformance target. Maintain good contrast and readable font sizes as baseline. Support reduced motion preferences where motion is used. Default language is English with Chinese content supported.
